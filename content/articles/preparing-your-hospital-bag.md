Indian hospitals vary more than any list can cover. A large private hospital will provide gowns, baby clothes for the stay, nappies, and toiletries, and will tell you at registration what to bring. A smaller nursing home or a government hospital may provide almost nothing. Ask at the pre-admission visit, and pack for the second case if you are not sure.

Plan for a two-night stay after a normal delivery and four to five after a caesarean. Two bags is a sensible split: one for labour and the first hours, one for the stay.

## The documents — the pocket that matters

- The antenatal file: every scan report and blood test, in date order.
- Hospital registration papers and the doctor's admission letter, if given.
- Insurance card and the TPA pre-authorisation, or the cash arrangement.
- Both of your ID cards (Aadhaar or passport), needed for the birth registration.
- Your blood group card.
- A written birth plan, if you have one, and the phone numbers of the people to call.

## For labour and the first hours

- A loose cotton nightdress or kurta that opens at the front, and a dupatta or shawl.
- Rubber slippers that slip on; socks — labour rooms are cold.
- A hair tie, lip balm, a water bottle with a straw, and something to eat afterwards — you will be hungrier than you expect.
- Phone, a long charging cable, and a power bank.
- For your partner: a change of clothes, snacks, and the list of who to call in what order.

## For the stay

- Two or three front-opening nightdresses; a light dressing gown.
- Nursing bras (a size up from now) and breast pads.
- Maternity pads — the thick kind, two packs; the hospital's supply runs out on day two.
- Old, large, soft cotton underwear — six or more — and after a caesarean, high-waisted ones that sit above the cut.
- Toiletries, a towel, and a small mirror; a comfortable going-home outfit that fitted at six months.
- For the baby: three or four cotton jhablas or bodysuits, mittens, a cap, two soft cotton sheets and a light blanket, a pack of newborn nappies, and cotton wool or fragrance-free wipes. The going-home outfit in a separate bag.

## What nobody uses

The birthing ball you will not be allowed in the labour room; the playlist; the second bag of baby clothes; the massage oil; the fancy robe. A book, perhaps — labour has long slow stretches — but the phone usually does that job. What people wish they had brought: a second phone charger, more pads, a small pillow of their own, and snacks for the night.

## The paperwork after the birth

Two documents come out of the hospital that families commonly lose: the discharge summary, which every paediatrician will ask for at the first visit and which records the birth weight, the Apgar scores and the vaccines given; and the immunisation card, which the baby will need for school admission years from now. Photograph both before you leave. The birth certificate is applied for at the municipal office, usually within 21 days, using the hospital's birth report — the hospital will tell you whether they file it or you do, and the answer varies by city.

If you are claiming maternity insurance, the claim needs the discharge summary, the itemised bill, and the pre-authorisation; keep them together in the same file that held the antenatal reports. That file, started in the first trimester, is now the child's first medical record.

## If it is a planned caesarean

Pack for four nights rather than two, add high-waisted underwear and a loose kurta that will not press on the wound, and a small pillow to hold against the abdomen when you cough or laugh. You will be asked to fast from midnight and to come in early; a phone charger and something to read matter more that morning than on a labour day.

> [!urgent] Do not wait for the bag if
> Your waters break, there is bleeding, contractions are five minutes apart or less, or the baby is moving much less. Go, and send someone back for the bag. A hospital can lend everything in it; it cannot lend the hour.

## Questions people ask

### When should the bag be packed?

By 36 weeks — earlier if you have had a preterm birth, are carrying twins, or have been told the baby may come early.

### Should I bring a car seat?

If you will drive home, yes — fitted in the car in advance, not carried up to the ward. Indian hospitals do not require one for discharge, but it is the safest way home.

### What about the baby's first vaccines?

BCG, the birth dose of hepatitis B and OPV are given before discharge in most hospitals; you do not bring them. Ask for the immunisation card before you leave.
