How to read a semen report

A semen report is full of terms and numbers, and a single flag in red can feel like a verdict. It is not. Sperm change from month to month, and one report is a snapshot. Here is what each line means, in plain words. Your doctor reads the whole report together with your history.

Lower reference limits are from the WHO laboratory manual for the examination of human semen, 6th edition (2021). They describe the lowest values seen in men whose partners conceived within a year. They are not pass marks. A value below a limit does not mean a man cannot father a child, and values above every limit do not mean there is no problem. Some labs still print the older WHO 2010 (5th edition) values, which differ a little. Check which your lab used.

Words you may see

  • Normozoospermia: the main values are at or above the reference limits.
  • Oligozoospermia: sperm number below the limit.
  • Asthenozoospermia: motility below the limit.
  • Teratozoospermia: normal forms below the limit.
  • Azoospermia: no sperm seen, even after the sample is spun down. This needs a specialist, and often has a cause that can be looked into.
  • Leucocytospermia: more white cells than expected.

Before and after the test

  • Follow the lab's abstinence advice, usually 2 to 7 days.
  • Collect the whole sample in the lab's container. Tell the lab if any was lost.
  • If collected at home, reach the lab within about an hour, keeping the container close to your body.
  • Tell the doctor about any fever, illness or new medicine in the last 3 months.
  • Doctors often ask for a repeat test before drawing conclusions.
  • If the report is not normal, ask about seeing a urologist or andrologist.
