NICU to home checklist

After days or weeks of monitors and nurses, bringing your baby home can feel both wonderful and frightening. That is normal. Use the days before discharge to learn, practise and ask. The NICU team wants you to feel ready, so ask them to show you again as many times as you need.

Learn before you leave

  • Feeding: breastfeeding, expressing milk, and paladai, spoon or bottle feeding if used
  • How much and how often to feed, and how to tell the baby is getting enough
  • Burping, and holding the baby upright after feeds
  • Kangaroo care (skin to skin), and how long to do it each day
  • Checking temperature with a thermometer
  • Keeping the baby warm without overheating
  • Cord care and nappy care
  • Sponging or bathing, if the team says it is time
  • Giving each medicine and drop: dose, time, how to measure
  • Safe sleep: on the back, on a firm flat surface, nothing loose nearby
  • What to do if the baby chokes or stops breathing, if the team offers training

Papers to collect

  • Discharge summary
  • Weight, length and head size at discharge
  • Medicine list with doses (for example iron, vitamin D, multivitamin)
  • Vaccination card, with birth doses marked
  • Hearing test result, or the date it is due
  • Eye check (ROP) result, or the date it is due
  • Newborn screening results (such as thyroid), or when they will come

Ask your doctor whether to track milestones by "corrected age" (counted from your due date) and how vaccines should be timed for your baby.

Get home ready

  • A clean, warm room, away from smoke: no smoking, mosquito coils or agarbatti near the baby
  • Soap and water, or sanitiser, near the door. Everyone washes hands first.
  • Few visitors for now. No one with a cough, cold or fever.
  • Thermometer, clean cloths, nappies, warm caps and socks
  • Medicines bought and a dropper or syringe to measure them
  • Safe travel home: a car seat if you have a car, or the baby held close and wrapped
  • NICU and doctor numbers saved in every phone

Go to hospital now if your baby

  • Breathes fast or with effort, grunts, or the chest pulls in
  • Has pauses in breathing, or blue or grey lips
  • Is not feeding well, or is too sleepy to feed
  • Feels hot with fever, or cold to touch
  • Has a fit, or goes stiff or floppy
  • Has yellow palms or soles
  • Vomits everything, or the vomit is green
  • Has far fewer wet nappies, or redness or pus around the cord
