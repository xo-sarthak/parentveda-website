High-risk pregnancy visit plan

"High-risk" is a label that tells your care team to watch more closely. It is not a prediction. Closer care means more visits, more tests and more dates to remember. This sheet keeps them in one place. Your doctor sets your own plan, so cross out what does not apply and add what does.

WeeksWhat usually happensOften added in a high-risk pregnancyDate done
6 to 10First visit. Dating scan. Blood tests: CBC, blood group and Rh, TSH, sugar (fasting or HbA1c), HIV, HBsAg, VDRL, urine routine. Thalassemia screen.Review of all your medicines. A plan for BP, sugar or thyroid. Ask whether low-dose aspirin or other medicines are right for you.
11 to 14NT scan with double marker test.Your doctor may check blood flow to the uterus or plan early screening for preeclampsia.
15 to 20Quadruple marker, if advised. Td (tetanus) injections as scheduled.Cervical length scan, if you have had early births or a stitch before.
18 to 22Anomaly scan (also called level 2 scan or TIFFA).Fetal echo (heart scan) if advised.
24 to 28OGTT (75 g glucose test). Repeat CBC. Anti-D injection around 28 weeks if you are Rh negative.Earlier sugar test if you are at higher risk. BP and urine protein at every visit.
28 to 32Visits every 2 to 4 weeks. Growth scan if advised. Start noticing your baby's movements each day.Growth and Doppler scans. Steroid injections for the baby's lungs, if early birth is likely.
32 to 36Visits every 2 weeks. Plan where you will deliver.Regular growth scans, Doppler and NST (baby's heart trace).
36 onwardWeekly visits. Talk about timing and type of birth.A planned date for delivery, if your doctor advises one.

Go to hospital now if

  • Bleeding from the vagina.
  • Fluid leaking or gushing from the vagina.
  • Your baby is moving less than usual.
  • Bad headache, blurred vision, or pain high in the belly.
  • Sudden swelling of face, hands or feet.
  • Regular pains or tightening before 37 weeks.
  • Fever, fits, or trouble breathing.
