What to bring to a fertility specialist

A first visit can feel like a lot. Walking in with your papers in order means less time spent remembering dates, and more time for your questions. Tick off what you have. Bring the originals and one set of photocopies. If you do not have a report yet, that is fine. The doctor will tell you what is needed.

Her reports

  • Hormone tests: AMH, and FSH and LH (usually done on day 2 or 3 of the period)
  • Thyroid (TSH) and prolactin
  • Sugar test: fasting sugar or HbA1c
  • CBC (complete blood count) and blood group
  • Rubella IgG, if done
  • Pelvic ultrasound reports, including any follicle study
  • HSG or other tube test report, with films or CD
  • Laparoscopy or hysteroscopy notes, with photos if given

His reports

  • Semen analysis (all reports, with dates)
  • Any hormone tests or scrotal ultrasound
  • Notes from a urologist or andrologist, if seen

For both

  • Infection tests, if already done: HIV, HBsAg (hepatitis B), anti-HCV (hepatitis C), VDRL
  • Thalassemia screen (CBC, HbA2 by HPLC), if done
  • Photo ID and any insurance papers

Your history, written down

  • How long you have been trying
  • First day of your last 3 to 6 periods, and how long your cycles usually are
  • Past pregnancies, miscarriages or ectopic pregnancy, with dates
  • Past surgeries, for either partner
  • Long-term conditions: thyroid, sugar, BP, PCOS, endometriosis
  • All medicines and supplements you each take now
  • Family history: early menopause, genetic conditions, thalassemia

Earlier treatment

  • Ovulation tablets or injections: names, doses, number of cycles
  • IUI: number of cycles and dates
  • IVF or ICSI: the clinic's summary, egg and embryo numbers, transfer dates
  • Details of any frozen embryos or eggs, and where they are stored

Questions to ask

  • What do our reports show so far? What is still missing?
  • Which tests do we need next, and why?
  • Which treatment options fit us, and what does each involve?
  • How long should we follow one plan before we review it?
  • What will it cost, and what is included?
  • Is there anything we can do at home: food, weight, sleep, habits?
  • Who do we call between visits if we have a question?

One small tip

Make a one-page timeline: when you started trying, each test, each treatment, and what happened. Doctors find it easier to read than a stack of reports, and it saves you answering the same questions twice. Go together if you can. Fertility is a question for both partners.
