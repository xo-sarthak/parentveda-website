Vegetarian diet chart for pregnancy sugar

Being told you have sugar in pregnancy can make every meal feel like a test. It does not have to. This is a sample week of everyday Indian vegetarian food, eaten as smaller meals through the day. Your dietitian or doctor sets the exact amounts for you, based on your sugar readings, weight and medicines.

Every day: Early morning, tea or milk without sugar, with 4 to 5 soaked almonds or 2 walnut halves. At bedtime, 1 cup of milk without sugar, or the snack your dietitian suggests. Drink water through the day.

Roti means a medium phulka made from whole wheat or mixed atta. Katori means a small bowl that holds about 150 ml. Swap dishes between days as you like, keeping the same pattern. The roti and katori counts are a starting point only. Your dietitian may give you more or less.

Your plate, in household measures

  • Half the plate: sabzi and salad. They fill you up and go easy on sugar.
  • A quarter of the plate: protein. Dal, chana, rajma, paneer, curd, sprouts, soya.
  • A quarter of the plate: grain. Roti, rice, dalia or millets. Your dietitian tells you how many rotis or katoris are right for you.
  • Fruit: one whole fruit at a time, between meals. Not juice.
  • Oil and ghee: a little, measured with a teaspoon, not poured.

Habits that help

  • Eat small meals every 2 to 3 hours. Do not skip meals.
  • Eat rice or roti with dal, curd or sabzi, not on their own.
  • Take a short, easy walk after meals, if your doctor says it is safe for you.
  • Check your sugar at the times your doctor sets, and write each reading down.
  • Take any tablets or insulin exactly as prescribed.

Keep these for rare, small amounts

Sugar, gur, honey, mithai, fruit juice, cold drinks, sweet lassi, white bread, maida biscuits, namkeen, fried snacks, bakery items, and large helpings of rice or potato.

If you take insulin or sugar tablets

Ask your doctor what to do if your sugar drops too low. Signs can include shaking, sweating, a fast heartbeat, sudden hunger or feeling confused. Keep the snack your doctor suggests within reach.
