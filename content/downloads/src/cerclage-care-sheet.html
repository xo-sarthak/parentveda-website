Cervical stitch care sheet

A stitch in the cervix (cerclage) helps keep it closed while your baby grows. It is normal to feel careful, even a little scared, after it. This sheet keeps the everyday things in one place. Your doctor's advice for you comes first, always.

Go to hospital now if

  • You have bleeding from the vagina that is more than light spotting.
  • Fluid leaks or gushes from the vagina, even a small steady trickle.
  • You have cramps, period-like pain or tightening that comes and goes in a pattern.
  • You feel strong pressure low in the pelvis, as if the baby is pushing down.
  • You have fever, chills, or discharge that smells bad.
  • Your baby is moving less than usual (later in pregnancy).

Keep your hospital's number and your file ready. Do not wait to see if it settles.

The first few days

  • Light spotting and mild cramps can happen for a day or two. Tell your doctor if they do not settle.
  • Rest as advised. Ask how long, and what "rest" means for you: bed, home, or light work.
  • Take medicines exactly as prescribed. Do not stop any on your own.
  • Ask when your next check-up and scan are due.

Everyday care

  • Drink enough water through the day. It helps keep urine infections away.
  • Eat fibre (dal, sabzi, fruit, whole grains) so you do not strain on the toilet.
  • Avoid lifting heavy things: water buckets, gas cylinders, older children.
  • Ask your doctor before sex, long travel, or exercise.
  • Do not put anything in the vagina unless your doctor says so.
  • Some discharge is normal with a stitch. Note any change in colour or smell.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • How much rest do I need, and for how long?
  • Can I climb stairs, work, or travel?
  • Which medicines should I take, and until when?
  • How often will you check my cervix or do a scan?
  • When will the stitch be removed? Where will that happen?
  • What should I do if my waters break or pains start before then?
  • Which hospital should I go to in an emergency, day or night?

About removal

Most planned stitches are taken out near the end of pregnancy, often around 36 to 37 weeks, in the hospital. If labour starts or your waters break before that, the stitch needs to come out sooner, so go to hospital straight away. Your doctor will tell you the plan for your stitch.
